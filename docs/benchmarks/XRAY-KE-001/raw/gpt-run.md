## 04 — DISCREPANCY LEDGER

### D001 — 63 km vs 122 km

- **Claims:** C001
- **Sources:** Citizen/S004 versus S002/S003/S005
- **Classification:** `different_scope`

Citizen and Business Daily describe a **63-km road**. MyGov describes **122 km**.

The ESIA explicitly adds **54 km of spur roads**, while H. Young's Lot 3 description separately counts main road and feeder roads.

#### Reconciliation

The evidence strongly indicates that **63 km refers to the principal/main corridor**, while much larger figures include feeder/spur roads and associated works.

The apparent contradiction therefore largely dissolves when scope is separated.

However, one definitive current KeNHA schedule explicitly stating the current mainline-plus-spurs scope was not recovered.

### D002 — KSh 15.7bn vs 15.87bn vs 16.385bn vs 16.7bn

- **Claim:** C002
- **Classification:** `different_definition / different_date / unresolved`

Located values include:

- **KSh 15.7bn** — May 2025 MyGov project description.
- **KSh 15.87bn** — 2022 KeNHA-attributed contract sums and September 2026 ministry-attributed lot values.
- **KSh 16.385bn** — sum of Treasury November 2025 estimated costs.
- **KSh 16.7bn** — used in June 2026 parliamentary/KeNHA reporting and Citizen September 2026.

These cannot responsibly be collapsed into a single figure.

The records appear to mix at least:

**original contract sums ↔ estimated/revised project costs ↔ rounded public descriptions.**

No executed variation/revised-contract record was located establishing exactly how **KSh 16.7bn** is derived.

**Status: unresolved discrepancy.**

### D003 — “Most sections tarmacked” vs 20.2% / 34% / 28% completion

- **Claims:** C003, C005
- **Classification:** `different_definition OR genuine contradiction — unresolved at measurement level`

Citizen says most sections have already been tarmacked.

The September government-attributed status data puts the three lots at only **20.2%, 34%, and 28% completion**.

Those statements are in severe tension, but they are not automatically mathematical opposites.

A road can have asphalt placed over a substantial linear distance while still be incomplete because bridges, drainage, shoulders, interchanges, finishing works, utilities or other contractual works remain outstanding.

Therefore:

> Low overall physical completion does not mathematically prove that less than half the mainline has some tarmac.

However, the evidence located provides **no measurement supporting “most sections.”**

### D004 — June/July 2027 vs January 2028 completion expectations

- **Claim:** C005
- **Classification:** `revised_timeline`

May 2026 reporting described expectations around mid-2027.

By June 2026, a KeNHA delegation reportedly gave **January 2028** as the revised completion target, citing pending bills, slow funding and weather.

This is treated as evidence of a **revised project timeline**, not automatically as a contradiction.

### D005 — Historical contractor assignments versus later reporting

Earlier records assign:

- Lot 1 → China Railway No. 10
- Lot 2 → Sinohydro
- Lot 3 → H. Young

Some 2026 secondary reports contain inconsistent contractor names/assignments.

Because no primary novation/termination/re-award documents were located, no inference is made that the contractual parties formally changed.

**Classification:** `unresolved / probable secondary-source error or undocumented contractual change`.

## 06 — DISCONFIRMATION LOG

### C001 — 63 km

**Preliminary hypothesis:** Citizen's 63 km figure is correct.

**Counter-hypothesis:** The project is actually 122 km, making 63 km wrong.

**Strongest contrary evidence:** Government MyGov calls it 122 km.

**Result:** Hypothesis **survives but changes scope**.

The ESIA's separate spur-road programme and contractor scope records provide a credible explanation: different sources are counting different components.

**What would change the finding:** A current KeNHA design schedule showing that the main Mamboleo–Kipsitet carriageway itself is materially different from approximately 63 km.

### C002 — KSh 16.7bn

**Preliminary hypothesis:** KSh 16.7bn is the authoritative current project value.

**Counter-hypothesis:** KSh 16.7bn is a rounded/revised public figure unsupported by the underlying contract values.

**Strongest opposing evidence:** The original KeNHA-attributed contracts total roughly **KSh 15.87bn**; Treasury's later estimated costs total roughly **KSh 16.385bn**.

**Result:** **Weakened to unresolved exact value.**

KSh 16.7bn is repeatedly used in recent official-attributed reporting, so it cannot simply be dismissed.

The missing bridge is the record that turns the underlying values into exactly KSh 16.7bn.

**What would change the finding:** Approved revised contract values/variations totaling approximately KSh 16.7bn.

### C003 — Most sections already tarmacked

**Preliminary hypothesis:** More than half of the relevant road sections have been surfaced.

**Counter-hypothesis:** The article overstates physical delivery.

**Strongest opposing evidence:** Two days before publication, ministry-attributed status figures put Lots 1–3 at **20.2%, 34%, 28% overall completion**.

June's KeNHA-attributed overall figure was **28%**.

**Evidence favoring preliminary hypothesis:** Active asphalt/tarmacking works are documented in September.

**Result:** Preliminary hypothesis **does not survive as established fact**.

But the counter-hypothesis cannot be upgraded to “most sections are definitely not tarmacked,” because overall physical completion and linear surfacing are different metrics.

**What would change the finding:** Certified surfaced-kilometre data showing >50% of the relevant mainline had reached bitumen/asphalt standard.

### C004 — Presidential inspection

**Preliminary hypothesis:** The road formed part of Ruto's scheduled Kisumu inspections.

**Counter-hypothesis:** Citizen incorrectly inserted the project into the itinerary.

**Result:** **Survives.**

Contemporaneous itinerary reporting independently describes the planned road inspection.

What does **not** survive is stronger wording that he had already completed the inspection.

### C005 — Project substantially incomplete

**Preliminary hypothesis:** The road remained substantially incomplete immediately before the tour.

**Counter-hypothesis:** The status figures were stale and most work had since been completed.

**Strongest contrary evidence:** Reports of resumed tarmacking and visible construction progress.

**Strongest supporting evidence:** September 9–11 ministry-attributed figures of **20.2%, 34%, 28%**, following a June KeNHA-attributed overall figure of 28%.

**Result:** Hypothesis **survives strongly**.

No credible evidence located showed the project approaching overall completion by September 13.
